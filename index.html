Adaptive path planning on unstructured roads

A running prototype of the loop: sense, detect, track, predict, plan, control, replan. Everything on the canvas is computed live — noisy detections, an alpha–beta tracker, constant-velocity prediction with growing uncertainty, and a space–time A* planner that re-solves whenever the road changes.

Decision log

    What each block does here

    SenseRange and detection probability depend on which sensors are on and on weather. Turn all three off to see the perception-failure fallback.
    DetectMeasurements arrive at 10 Hz with Gaussian position noise; fusing more sensors lowers the noise.
    TrackAlpha–beta filter per road user, giving smoothed position and velocity. Tracks are dropped after 1.5 s without a detection.
    PredictConstant-velocity rollout over 4 s, with an uncertainty radius that grows with horizon.
    PlanA* over a 1 m grid where a cell is blocked at the time the vehicle would reach it, then shortcutting and smoothing.
    ControlPure pursuit steering with yaw-rate and acceleration limits.
    ReplanEvery 400 ms, and immediately when the safety layer finds the current path conflicting.

    Honest limits of this prototype

    The world model here is simulated, not sensed from real data — object detection is stubbed with a noise model rather than a trained network, and the vehicle uses kinematic motion, not dynamics with tyre slip. Numbers shown are properties of this simulation, not measured performance of a deployable system. The point it demonstrates is the architecture and the replanning behaviour under sudden changes.

    Next step in the plan: swap the stubbed detector for a model trained on the India Driving Dataset, and port the same planner into MATLAB/Simulink with RoadRunner Scenario for repeatable scenario batches.
